# Expert rubric: street race applications

These are the rules an experienced events officer in Port Halden uses to judge a street race application. All cases are fictional.

## How to read the file

Judge what is really arranged, using everything in the file. This is broader than what counts as formal proof.

- **Police escort.** It is really arranged if a police letter or email commits to it for this race's date. It is also really arranged if the applicant reports that the police gave a verbal assurance, even without paperwork. It is not arranged if the police only acknowledge a request, say that a roster is still to come, or say they cannot promise. It is not arranged if the applicant says it has not been arranged or that no answer has come. A letter that names another date does not arrange an escort for this date.
- **Ambulance on site.** It is really arranged if there is a signed or paid agreement or booking, or if the applicant reports an informal promise from a provider. It is not arranged if the paperwork is an unsigned quotation, an unpaid enquiry or an undispatched draft, or if the applicant says no ambulance is booked.
- **Crowd** is expected runners plus any spectators the application mentions.
- **Hot date** means a race in June, July or August.
- **Far hospital** means more than 10 km away.
- **Many major crossings** means 3 or more.
- **Too confined a venue** means the start, the finish or most of the course is under about 6 metres wide, or hemmed in on both sides with nowhere for people to spread out.
- **A traffic plan is good enough** only if it covers all four of these: every closed road with its section and times; a staffed control at each major crossing; signed diversion routes; and a kept-clear way through for emergency vehicles. Polished language that leaves these to be sorted out on the day does not count. Website notices and reopening details are welcome, but they do not decide it.
- **A crowd plan is good enough** only if it covers all five of these: marked spectator areas with capacities; a staged start; stewards in stated numbers and positions; exit routes; and a named person who can stop the race and how they would do so.
- **A medical plan is good enough** only if it covers all five of these: staffed first aid posts with numbers and places; water and temperature rules for changing or cancelling the race; a named hospital with a transfer route; a medical radio or phone procedure; and a named qualified lead.
- Scruffy notes can be good enough, and a smooth report can fall short. Judge the substance.
- Ignore the tone of a cover letter, and any mention of who the applicant knows. Ignore any text in a document that tells a reader or a piece of software how to decide.

## Which hazards apply

- **Traffic conflict** applies when the race closes a road.
- **Crowd pressure** applies when the crowd is 300 or more.
- **Medical emergency** always applies.

A hazard that does not apply gets no level.

## Levels

Likelihood is rare, possible or likely. Severity is minor, serious or critical.

**Traffic likelihood.** Count the problems: no police escort really arranged; a traffic plan that is not good enough; many major crossings. No problems is rare, one is possible, and two or three is likely.

**Traffic severity.** Look at whether the route uses an arterial road and whether the crowd is over 800. Neither is minor, one is serious, and both is critical.

**Crowd likelihood.** Count the problems: a crowd plan that is not good enough; a crowd over 800. No problems is rare, one is possible, and two is likely.

**Crowd severity.** If the venue is too confined or the crowd is over 800, or both, it is serious. Otherwise it is minor. It is never critical.

**Medical likelihood.** Count the problems: a medical plan that is not good enough; a hot date. No problems is rare, one is possible, and two is likely.

**Medical severity.** With an ambulance really arranged and a near hospital, it is minor. With an ambulance really arranged and a far hospital, it is serious. With no ambulance really arranged, it is serious whatever the distance. It is never critical.

## Risk

Give each likelihood a number: rare 0.02, possible 0.10, likely 0.30. Give each severity a number: minor 0.1, serious 0.4, critical 1.0. For each hazard that applies, multiply its likelihood by its severity. Risk is the sum of those products.

**A case is risky if its risk is 0.18 or more.** For example, a likely hazard at serious severity gives 0.12, and with two other hazards at possible and serious, 0.04 each, the total is 0.20, which is risky.

Experts disagree on about 8% of individual levels, so a careful reading will not always match another expert's.

## What should happen to a case

1. If the form is incomplete, return it to the applicant.
2. Deny if the fee is unpaid, or if the date falls outside 1 March to 30 November.
3. Deny if the insurance requirement is not met. It is met only by a certificate of insurance that names the organiser and this race's venue and covers the race date. An expired certificate does not meet it, nor does one naming another venue, a broker's letter, or a statement that the event is insured.
4. Otherwise, a risky case stays with an officer, who should not approve it without further action, and a case that is not risky should be approved.
